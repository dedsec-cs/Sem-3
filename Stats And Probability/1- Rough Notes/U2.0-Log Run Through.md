## 📚 Logarithms: Quick Review

A **logarithm** is simply the inverse operation to exponentiation. It answers the question: "To what power must we raise the base to get a certain number?"

### The Basic Relationship
The relationship between exponents and logarithms is:

$$b^x = y \iff \log_b(y) = x$$

* **Example:** $10^2 = 100$ is equivalent to $\log_{10}(100) = 2$.

In statistics and calculus, we primarily use two bases:
1.  **Base 10 (Common Log):** $\log_{10}(y)$ is often written as $\log(y)$.
2.  **Base $e$ (Natural Log):** $\log_e(y)$ is written as $\mathbf{\ln(y)}$. **This is the one you will use most often for linearization.** ($e \approx 2.718$)

## 🔑 The Four Key Log Rules for Curve Fitting

These rules allow you to simplify complicated multiplicative or exponential models into a simple linear form.

### 1. Product Rule (Breaks Multiplication into Addition)
The log of a product is the sum of the logs.
$$\log_b(M \cdot N) = \log_b(M) + \log_b(N)$$

| Use Case | Transformation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Model:** $\hat{y} = A \cdot x^B$ | **$\ln(\hat{y}) = \ln(A) + \ln(x^B)$** |

### 2. Quotient Rule (Breaks Division into Subtraction)
The log of a quotient is the difference of the logs.
$$\log_b\left(\frac{M}{N}\right) = \log_b(M) - \log_b(N)$$

| Use Case | Transformation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Model:** $\hat{y} = \frac{A}{x^B}$ | **$\ln(\hat{y}) = \ln(A) - \ln(x^B)$** |

### 3. Power Rule (Brings Exponents Down)
The log of a number raised to a power is the power multiplied by the log of the number. **This is the most critical rule for linearization.**
$$\log_b(M^k) = k \cdot \log_b(M)$$

| Use Case | Transformation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Model:** $\hat{y} = x^B$ | $\ln(\hat{y}) = B \cdot \ln(x)$ |

### 4. Special Case: Log of the Base
The log of the base raised to a power simplifies directly to the exponent.
$$\log_b(b^x) = x \quad \text{or} \quad \mathbf{\ln(e^x) = x}$$

| Use Case | Transformation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Model:** $\hat{y} = A \cdot e^{Bx}$ | $\ln(\hat{y}) = \ln(A) + \ln(e^{Bx})$ **$\implies \ln(\hat{y}) = \ln(A) + Bx$** |

## 🛠️ The Core Strategy: Linearization

For curve fitting, you often encounter two non-linear models that can be made linear using logs:

### Case A: Exponential Model
* **Non-Linear Model:** $\hat{y} = A e^{Bx}$
    * Parameters $A$ and $B$ are non-linear.
* **Goal:** Turn this into the linear form $Y = a + bX$.
* **Action:** Take the **Natural Log ($\ln$)** of both sides.
    $$\ln(\hat{y}) = \ln(A e^{Bx})$$
    $$\ln(\hat{y}) = \ln(A) + \ln(e^{Bx}) \quad \text{(Product Rule)}$$
    $$\mathbf{\ln(\hat{y}) = \ln(A) + Bx} \quad \text{(Special Case } \ln(e^x)=x)$$
* **Result (Linear Form):**
    $$\text{Let } Y = \ln(\hat{y}), \quad a = \ln(A), \quad b = B, \quad X = x$$
    $$\mathbf{Y = a + bx}$$
    You can now use the standard Straight Line Normal Equations to solve for $a$ and $b$.

### Case B: Power Model
* **Non-Linear Model:** $\hat{y} = A x^B$
    * Parameters $A$ and $B$ are non-linear.
* **Goal:** Turn this into the linear form $Y = a + bX$.
* **Action:** Take the **Natural Log ($\ln$)** of both sides.
    $$\ln(\hat{y}) = \ln(A x^B)$$
    $$\ln(\hat{y}) = \ln(A) + \ln(x^B) \quad \text{(Product Rule)}$$
    $$\mathbf{\ln(\hat{y}) = \ln(A) + B \ln(x)} \quad \text{(Power Rule)}$$
* **Result (Linear Form):**
    $$\text{Let } Y = \ln(\hat{y}), \quad a = \ln(A), \quad b = B, \quad X = \ln(x)$$
    $$\mathbf{Y = a + bX}$$
    You can now use the standard Straight Line Normal Equations to solve for $a$ and $b$, but you must transform both your $x$ and $y$ data first.
