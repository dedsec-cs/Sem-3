## Z-Test Explanation

The **Z-test** is a type of hypothesis test used to determine if the means of two data sets are significantly different from each other, or if a single sample mean is significantly different from a hypothesized population mean. It is specifically based on the **Normal Distribution**.

---

### When to Apply the Z-Test

You should use a Z-test when you can satisfy one of the following two primary conditions:

| Condition | Description | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Large Sample Size** | The sample size ($n$) is **greater than or equal to 30** ($n \ge 30$). | The **Central Limit Theorem** states that for large samples, the distribution of the sample mean approaches the Normal Distribution, regardless of the population's original shape. |
| **2. Known Population Standard Deviation** | The true standard deviation of the **population ($\sigma$)** is **known**, even if the sample size is small. | When $\sigma$ is known, you use the standard normal distribution (Z-distribution) for the test. (In reality, this is rare, making the large sample size rule the most common reason for using the Z-test). |

> **Contrast with T-test:** If the sample size is small ($n < 30$) AND the population standard deviation ($\sigma$) is unknown, you must use the **T-test**, which relies on the $t$-distribution to account for the added uncertainty.

---

### How to Apply the Z-Test (One-Sample Mean)

The application of the Z-test follows the general procedure for hypothesis testing. Here is the process for testing a single population mean.

#### 1. State the Hypotheses
* **Null Hypothesis ($H_0$):** The population mean ($\mu$) is equal to the hypothesized value ($\mu_0$). $H_0: \mu = \mu_0$.
* **Alternate Hypothesis ($H_1$):** The population mean is different from the hypothesized value. This determines the type of test:
    * **Two-tailed:** $H_1: \mu \ne \mu_0$
    * **Right-tailed:** $H_1: \mu > \mu_0$
    * **Left-tailed:** $H_1: \mu < \mu_0$

#### 2. Choose the Level of Significance ($\alpha$)
This is the maximum risk of rejecting a true null hypothesis, commonly set at $\alpha = 0.05$ (5%) or $\alpha = 0.01$ (1%).

#### 3. Calculate the Test Statistic ($Z_{\text{Calculated}}$)
The $Z$-test statistic measures the difference between your sample mean ($\bar{x}$) and the hypothesized population mean ($\mu$) in units of the standard error.

The formula for the **One-Sample Z-test** is:
$$Z_{\text{Calculated}} =\frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}}$$

Where:
* $\bar{x}$: Sample Mean
* $\mu_0$: Hypothesized Population Mean (from $H_0$)
* $\sigma$: Population Standard Deviation (Use the Sample Standard Deviation $S$ if $\sigma$ is unknown and $n \ge 30$)
* $n$: Sample Size

#### 4. Determine the Critical Value ($Z_{\text{Critical}}$)
Look up the critical $Z$-value in the Standard Normal Distribution table based on your chosen $\alpha$ and the type of test (one or two-tailed).

| $\alpha$ | Two-Tailed ($Z = \pm$) | One-Tailed ($Z$ only) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0.05 (5%)** | $\pm 1.96$ | $\pm 1.645$ |
| **0.01 (1%)** | $\pm 2.58$ | $\pm 2.33$ |

#### 5. Make a Conclusion (Critical Value Approach)
Compare the absolute value of $Z_{\text{Calculated}}$ to the absolute value of $Z_{\text{Critical}}$.

* **Reject $H_0$:** If $|Z_{\text{Calculated}}| > |Z_{\text{Critical}}|$, the result is **statistically significant**, and the difference is too large to be explained by chance.
* **Accept $H_0$ (Fail to Reject $H_0$):** If $|Z_{\text{Calculated}}| \le |Z_{\text{Critical}}|$, the result falls into the acceptance region, and there is not enough evidence to reject the claim ($\mu = \mu_0$).

---

## What is P-Value

The **P-value** (Probability Value) is an alternative way to make the final conclusion in a hypothesis test, providing a more precise measure of the evidence against the Null Hypothesis ($H_0$).

### Simple Definition
The P-value is the probability of obtaining a test statistic **as extreme as**, or **more extreme than**, the one you calculated from your sample data, **assuming that the Null Hypothesis ($H_0$) is actually true.**

### Interpretation
Think of the P-value as the measure of surprise:

* **A Small P-value:** Means the observed sample result is **highly unlikely** to occur if $H_0$ were true. This is strong evidence *against* $H_0$.
* **A Large P-value:** Means the observed sample result is **likely** to occur if $H_0$ were true. This is weak evidence *against* $H_0$.

### Decision Rule (P-Value Approach)

Instead of comparing the calculated statistic to a critical value, you compare the P-value to the pre-chosen Level of Significance ($\alpha$).

| P-value vs. $\alpha$ | Decision | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **P-value $\le \alpha$** | **Reject the Null Hypothesis ($H_0$)** | The chance of observing your data (if $H_0$ were true) is less than the risk of error ($\alpha$) you were willing to accept. |
| **P-value $> \alpha$** | **Accept the Null Hypothesis ($H_0$)** (or Fail to Reject) | The chance of observing your data (if $H_0$ were true) is higher than the risk of error ($\alpha$) you were willing to accept. |

> **Example:** If you set $\alpha = 0.05$ and your test yields a P-value of $0.01$: Since $0.01 \le 0.05$, you **Reject $H_0$**.

## Hypothesis Testing (Z-test/Large Sample Tests) Examples

---

### Example 1: Testing a Single Population Mean (Known $\sigma$)

**Question:** A random sample of **900 members** has a mean **3.4 cm**. Can it be reasonably regarded as a sample from a large population of mean **3.2 cm** and S.D. **2.3 cm**? (Test at **5% level of significance**)

#### 1. State the Data and Hypotheses
* Sample Size: $n = 900$ (Large Sample, $n > 30$)
* Sample Mean: $\bar{x} = 3.4$ cm
* Hypothesized Population Mean: $\mu_0 = 3.2$ cm
* Population Standard Deviation: $\sigma = 2.3$ cm
* Level of Significance: $\alpha = 0.05$

Since the question asks if the sample **can be regarded as** from the population, we test for any difference (it could be greater or smaller), leading to a **Two-Tailed Test**.

* **Null Hypothesis ($H_0$):** $\mu = 3.2$ (The sample comes from the population; no difference.)
* **Alternate Hypothesis ($H_1$):** $\mu \ne 3.2$ (The sample does **not** come from the population; there is a difference.)

#### 2. Determine the Critical Value (Two-Tailed)
For $\alpha = 0.05$ in a two-tailed Z-test, the critical values are:
$$Z_{\text{Critical}} = \pm 1.96$$

#### 3. Calculate the Test Statistic ($Z_{\text{Calculated}}$)
The formula is $Z =\frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}}$:

$$Z_{\text{Calculated}} = \frac{3.4 - 3.2}{\frac{2.3}{\sqrt{900}}} = \frac{0.2}{\frac{2.3}{30}} = \frac{0.2}{0.0767} \approx 2.6076$$

#### 4. Conclusion

* **Critical Value Approach:**
    * $|Z_{\text{Calculated}}| = 2.6076$
    * $|Z_{\text{Critical}}| = 1.96$
    * Since $2.6076 > 1.96$, the calculated $Z$ falls in the rejection region.

* **Result:** **Reject $H_0$**.
* **Interpretation:** The sample mean of $3.4$ cm is **significantly different** from the hypothesized population mean of $3.2$ cm at the $5\%$ level. It **cannot** be reasonably regarded as a sample from that population.

### Example 2: Testing a Single Population Mean (Unknown $\sigma$, Calculated from Data)

**Question:** An insurance agent claims the avg. age of policy holders is **less than** the avg. age of all agents, which is **30.5 yrs**. A sample of **100 policy holders** gave the following age distribution. Calculate the mean and S.D. and test his claim at **5% level of significance**.

* $n = 100$ (Large Sample, $n > 30$)
* Hypothesized Population Mean: $\mu_0 = 30.5$ years
* Claim: Average age is **less than** $30.5$ years (This is a **Left-Tailed Test**).
* Level of Significance: $\alpha = 0.05$

#### 1. Calculate Sample Mean ($\bar{x}$) and Sample Standard Deviation ($S$)

We need to calculate $\bar{x}$ and $S$ from the grouped data.

| Age Group | Midpoint ($x$) | Frequency ($f$) | $f \cdot x$ | $x^2$ | $f \cdot x^2$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 16-20 | 18 | 12 | 216 | 324 | 3888 |
| 21-25 | 23 | 22 | 506 | 529 | 11638 |
| 26-30 | 28 | 20 | 560 | 784 | 15680 |
| 31-35 | 33 | 30 | 990 | 1089 | 32670 |
| 36-40 | 38 | 16 | 608 | 1444 | 23104 |
| **Total** | | $\mathbf{\sum f = 100}$ | $\mathbf{\sum fx = 2880}$ | | $\mathbf{\sum fx^2 = 86980}$ |

* **Sample Mean ($\bar{x}$):**
    $$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum fx}{n} = \frac{2880}{100} = \mathbf{28.8} \text{ years}$$
* **Sample Variance ($S^2$):**
    $$S^2 = \frac{\sum fx^2}{n} - (\bar{x})^2 = \frac{86980}{100} - (28.8)^2 = 869.8 - 829.44 = 40.36$$
* **Sample Standard Deviation ($S$):**
    $$S = \sqrt{40.36} \approx \mathbf{6.353} \text{ years}$$

#### 2. State Hypotheses and Critical Value
* **Null Hypothesis ($H_0$):** $\mu = 30.5$
* **Alternate Hypothesis ($H_1$):** $\mu < 30.5$ (Left-Tailed Test, supporting the agent's claim)

For $\alpha = 0.05$ in a left-tailed Z-test, the critical value is:
$$Z_{\text{Critical}} = -1.645$$

#### 3. Calculate the Test Statistic ($Z_{\text{Calculated}}$)
Since $n$ is large, we use $S$ as an estimate for $\sigma$: $\sigma \approx S = 6.353$.

$$Z_{\text{Calculated}} = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{\frac{S}{\sqrt{n}}} = \frac{28.8 - 30.5}{\frac{6.353}{\sqrt{100}}} = \frac{-1.7}{\frac{6.353}{10}} = \frac{-1.7}{0.6353} \approx -2.676$$

#### 4. Conclusion

* **Critical Value Approach:**
    * $Z_{\text{Calculated}} = -2.676$
    * $Z_{\text{Critical}} = -1.645$
    * Since $-2.676$ is to the left of $-1.645$, it falls in the rejection region.

* **Result:** **Reject $H_0$**.
* **Interpretation:** The average age of the policy holders who insured through the agent ($\bar{x}=28.8$) is **significantly less** than $30.5$ years. The agent's claim is **supported** at the $5\%$ level of significance.

### Example 3: Testing the Difference Between Two Population Means

**Question:** Sample 1: $n_1=100$ people, $\bar{x}_1=210$, $S_1=10$. Sample 2: $n_2=150$ persons, $\bar{x}_2=220$, $S_2=12$. Test whether there is any significance difference between the avg. income of the localities. (Test at **5% level of significance**)

#### 1. State the Data and Hypotheses
* Sample 1 (Locality 1): $n_1=100$, $\bar{x}_1=210$, $S_1=10$
* Sample 2 (Locality 2): $n_2=150$, $\bar{x}_2=220$, $S_2=12$
* Both samples are large ($n_1, n_2 > 30$).
* Level of Significance: $\alpha = 0.05$

Since the question asks if there is **any significance difference**, we use a **Two-Tailed Test**.

* **Null Hypothesis ($H_0$):** $\mu_1 = \mu_2$ or $\mu_1 - \mu_2 = 0$ (No difference in average income.)
* **Alternate Hypothesis ($H_1$):** $\mu_1 \ne \mu_2$ or $\mu_1 - \mu_2 \ne 0$ (There is a difference.)

#### 2. Determine the Critical Value (Two-Tailed)
For $\alpha = 0.05$ in a two-tailed Z-test, the critical values are:
$$Z_{\text{Critical}} = \pm 1.96$$

#### 3. Calculate the Test Statistic ($Z_{\text{Calculated}}$)
The formula for the $Z$-test for the difference between two means (using $S_1$ and $S_2$ as estimates for $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$):

$$Z_{\text{Calculated}} = \frac{(\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2) - (\mu_1 - \mu_2)_0}{\sqrt{\frac{S_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{S_2^2}{n_2}}}$$

Since $H_0$ assumes $\mu_1 - \mu_2 = 0$:

$$Z_{\text{Calculated}} = \frac{(210 - 220) - 0}{\sqrt{\frac{10^2}{100} + \frac{12^2}{150}}} = \frac{-10}{\sqrt{\frac{100}{100} + \frac{144}{150}}}$$

$$Z_{\text{Calculated}} = \frac{-10}{\sqrt{1.00 + 0.96}} = \frac{-10}{\sqrt{1.96}} = \frac{-10}{1.4} \approx -7.14$$

#### 4. Conclusion

* **Critical Value Approach:**
    * $|Z_{\text{Calculated}}| = 7.14$
    * $|Z_{\text{Critical}}| = 1.96$
    * Since $7.14 > 1.96$, the calculated $Z$ falls in the rejection region.

* **Result:** **Reject $H_0$**.
* **Interpretation:** There is a **highly significant difference** between the average incomes of the two localities at the $5\%$ level.

## Hypothesis Testing (P-value Approach) Example

**Question:** A manufacturing process produces TV tubes with an average life $\mu=1200$ hr and $\sigma=300$ hrs. A new process is thought to give tubes a **higher average life**. A sample of **100 tubes** has an average life $\overline{x}=1265$ hr. Is the new process really any better than the old?

#### 1. State the Data and Hypotheses
* Sample Size: $n = 100$
* Sample Mean: $\bar{x} = 1265$ hr
* Hypothesized Population Mean: $\mu_0 = 1200$ hr
* Population Standard Deviation: $\sigma = 300$ hr (Known $\sigma$, so Z-test is appropriate)
* Claim: Higher average life (This is a **Right-Tailed Test**).

* **Null Hypothesis ($H_0$):** $\mu = 1200$ (The new process is not better.)
* **Alternate Hypothesis ($H_1$):** $\mu > 1200$ (The new process is better.)

#### 2. Calculate the Test Statistic ($Z_{\text{Calculated}}$)

$$Z_{\text{Calculated}} = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}} = \frac{1265 - 1200}{\frac{300}{\sqrt{100}}} = \frac{65}{\frac{300}{10}} = \frac{65}{30} \approx 2.167$$

#### 3. Determine the P-Value
The P-value is the probability of observing a $Z$-score of $2.167$ or greater in a standard normal distribution. This is $P(Z \ge 2.167)$.

Using the Z-table:
* The area to the left of $Z=2.167$ is approximately $0.9845$.
* The area to the right (the P-value) is:
    $$P\text{-value} = 1 - 0.9845 = \mathbf{0.0155}$$

#### 4. Conclusion (P-Value Approach)

The problem implicitly uses $\alpha = 0.05$ for comparison.

* **Compare P-value to $\alpha$:**
    * $P\text{-value} = 0.0155$
    * $\alpha = 0.05$
    * Since $0.0155 \le 0.05$, the **P-value is less than $\alpha$**.

* **Result:** **Reject $H_0$**.
* **Interpretation:** The probability of getting a sample mean of $1265$ (or higher) by chance, if the true mean were still $1200$, is very low ($1.55\%$). Because $1.55\%$ is less than the accepted risk of $5\%$, we conclude that the new process **is really better** than the old one.

## Quiz and Multiple Choice Questions Solutions

### Quiz Question 1: Could the mean weight in the population be 120 pounds?
* $n=800$, $\bar{x}=112$, $S=20$, $\mu_0=120$, $\alpha=0.05$ (Two-Tailed $Z_{\text{Crit}} = \pm 1.96$)

1.  **Hypotheses:** $H_0: \mu = 120$ vs. $H_1: \mu \ne 120$.
2.  **$Z_{\text{Calculated}}$:**
    $$Z = \frac{112 - 120}{\frac{20}{\sqrt{800}}} = \frac{-8}{\frac{20}{28.28}} = \frac{-8}{0.707} \approx -11.315$$
3.  **Conclusion:** $|-11.315| > 1.96$. **Reject $H_0$**.
4.  **Answer:** No, the sample mean of 112 pounds is significantly different from 120 pounds.

### Quiz Question 2: Could the mean weight in the population be 120 pounds?
* $n=1000$, $\bar{x}=112$, $S=20$, $\mu_0=120$, $\alpha=0.05$ (Two-Tailed $Z_{\text{Crit}} = \pm 1.96$)

1.  **Hypotheses:** $H_0: \mu = 120$ vs. $H_1: \mu \ne 120$.
2.  **$Z_{\text{Calculated}}$:**
    $$Z = \frac{112 - 120}{\frac{20}{\sqrt{1000}}} = \frac{-8}{\frac{20}{31.62}} = \frac{-8}{0.632} \approx -12.65$$
3.  **Conclusion:** $|-12.65| > 1.96$. **Reject $H_0$**.
4.  **Answer:** No, the sample mean of 112 pounds is significantly different from 120 pounds. (Note: Increasing the sample size from 800 to 1000 made the difference even more statistically significant.)

### Quiz Question 3: Random sample of 900 members has mean 3.4cms. Population mean 3.2cms and S.D. 2.3 cms. Tabulated value 1.96 at 5%.

This question is identical to **Example 1**.
* $Z_{\text{Calculated}} \approx 2.6076$
* $Z_{\text{Tabulated/Critical}} = 1.96$

Since $2.6076 > 1.96$, the null hypothesis must be **rejected**.

* **Correct Result:** The calculated $Z$ value is outside the acceptance region, so the Null Hypothesis ($H_0: \mu = 3.2$) is **Rejected**.
