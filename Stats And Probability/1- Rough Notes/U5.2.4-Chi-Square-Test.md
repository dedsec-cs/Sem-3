## Chi-Square Test ($\chi^2$-Test)

The **Chi-Square Test** ($\chi^2$-Test) is a statistical tool primarily used to examine the relationship between two **categorical variables** or to compare an observed distribution with a theoretical one.

---

## Parametric vs. Nonparametric Tests

Statistical hypothesis tests are broadly divided into two categories based on the assumptions they make about the population data:

| Feature              | Parametric Tests (e.g., $Z$-test, $t$-test, $F$-test)              | Nonparametric Tests (e.g., $\chi^2$-test)                                           |
| :------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Data Type**        | Typically use **interval** or **ratio** data (measurable amounts). | Typically use **nominal** or **ordinal** data (categories or ranks).                |
| **Assumptions**      | Make specific assumptions about the population parameters:         | Make **fewer** or **no assumptions** about the population distribution or variance. |
|                      | 1. Data follows a **Normal Distribution**.                         | They are often called **distribution-free** tests.                                  |
|                      | 2. Assumes **Homogeneity of Variance**.                            |                                                                                     |
| **Central Tendency** | Based on **mean** ($\mu$).                                         | Often based on **median** or **mode** (counts/frequencies).                         |
| **Chi-Square Test**  | **$\chi^2$-test is Nonparametric.**                                |                                                                                     |

---

## How the Chi-Square Test Statistic is Computed

The $\chi^2$ test statistic is based on comparing the **Observed Frequencies** ($O$)—the counts actually recorded in the sample—with the **Expected Frequencies** ($E$)—the counts expected if the Null Hypothesis ($H_0$) were true.

### Formula

The test statistic is the sum of the squared differences between the observed and expected counts, divided by the expected counts, across all categories or cells:

$$\chi^2_{\text{Calculated}} = \sum \frac{(O - E)^2}{E}$$

Where:
* $O$: The **Observed Frequency** (actual count) in each category/cell.
* $E$: The **Expected Frequency** (theoretical count) in each category/cell.

A **small** $\chi^2$ value indicates that $O$ and $E$ are close, supporting $H_0$. A **large** $\chi^2$ value indicates a significant difference, leading to the rejection of $H_0$.

---

## Chi-Square Goodness-of-Fit Test

The **Goodness-of-Fit Test** determines if a sample distribution matches a hypothesized population distribution (e.g., checking if customer preferences are equally split among four flavors, or if student grades follow a known historical pattern).

### Procedure

1.  **State Hypotheses:**
    * **$H_0$:** The observed frequencies match the expected (theoretical) distribution.
    * **$H_1$:** The observed frequencies do not match the expected distribution.
2.  **Calculate Expected Frequencies ($E$):** $E$ is calculated based on the $H_0$ assumption (e.g., if $H_0$ claims equal distribution among 5 categories and the total sample is 100, $E = 100 / 5 = 20$ for each category).
3.  **Calculate $\chi^2$:** Use the formula $\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O - E)^2}{E}$.
4.  **Find Critical Value:** Use the $\chi^2$ table with the chosen $\alpha$ and **Degrees of Freedom** ($df = \text{number of categories} - 1$).
5.  **Conclusion:** If $\chi^2_{\text{Calculated}} > \chi^2_{\text{Critical}}$, **Reject $H_0$**.

---

## Chi-Square Test for Independence

The **Test for Independence** determines whether there is a statistically significant relationship between two **categorical variables** in a single population (e.g., is gender independent of voting preference?). The data is organized in a **contingency table**.

### Procedure

1.  **State Hypotheses:**
    * **$H_0$:** The two variables are **independent** (not related).
    * **$H_1$:** The two variables are **dependent** (related).
2.  **Calculate Expected Frequencies ($E$):** For each cell in the contingency table:
    $$E = \frac{(\text{Row Total}) \times (\text{Column Total})}{\text{Grand Total}}$$
3.  **Calculate $\chi^2$:** Use the formula $\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O - E)^2}{E}$ summed over all cells.
4.  **Find Critical Value:**
    * **Degrees of Freedom:** $df = (\text{Number of Rows} - 1) \times (\text{Number of Columns} - 1)$
5.  **Conclusion:** If $\chi^2_{\text{Calculated}} > \chi^2_{\text{Critical}}$, **Reject $H_0$**, concluding the variables are dependent.

---

## Assumptions and Restrictions

The validity of the $\chi^2$-test relies on the following key conditions:

### Assumptions

1.  **Categorical Data:** The data must be in the form of **frequencies** (counts) from distinct categories.
2.  **Random Sampling:** The sample must be drawn using a **random sampling** method.
3.  **Independence of Observations:** Each observation must be independent of every other observation (e.g., one person cannot fall into two different cells of the table).

### Restriction of Expected Frequency Size

The $\chi^2$ approximation is only reliable if the sample size is large enough to ensure that the expected frequencies ($E$) are not too small.

* **Rule:** **No more than 20%** of the expected frequencies ($E$) should be less than **5**.
* **Absolute Minimum:** **Every** expected frequency ($E$) should be **at least 1**.

If this restriction is violated, the categories must be combined (pooled) to increase the expected cell counts, or a different test must be used.

----

The Chi-Square ($\chi^2$) test uses observed counts to test two things: how well a sample distribution matches a theoretical distribution (Goodness-of-Fit) or whether two categorical variables are related (Test for Independence).

##  Hypothesis Testing (Chi-square test) Examples

### Example 1: Chi-Square Goodness-of-Fit Test (Equal Frequencies)

**Question:** The demand for a spare part varied daily. Sample data: 1124, 1125, 1110, 1120, 1126, 1115 (for Mon-Sat). Expected frequency ($E$) is 1118 for each day. **Test the hypothesis.** (Assume $\alpha=0.05$)

* Total Demand: $\sum O = 6720$ (The provided calculation of 1120 is incorrect; $6270/6 = 1045$ is also wrong. Using the data: $1124+1125+1110+1120+1126+1115 = 6720$).
* **Corrected Expected Frequency ($E$):** $E = 6720 / 6 = \mathbf{1120}$ for each of the 6 days.
* $n=6$ categories.

#### 1. Hypotheses and Critical Value
* **$H_0$:** The demand is uniformly distributed across the six days ($\text{Observed = Expected}$).
* **$H_1$:** The demand is not uniformly distributed.
* **Degrees of Freedom ($df$):** $df = \text{Categories} - 1 = 6 - 1 = 5$.
* **Critical Value ($\chi^2_{\text{Critical}}$):** For $df=5$ and $\alpha=0.05$, $\chi^2_{\text{Critical}} \approx \mathbf{11.07}$.

#### 2. Calculate the $\chi^2$ Statistic
| Day | Observed ($O$) | Expected ($E$) | $(O-E)$ | $(O-E)^2$ | $\frac{(O-E)^2}{E}$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mon | 1124 | 1120 | 4 | 16 | $16/1120 \approx 0.0143$ |
| Tue | 1125 | 1120 | 5 | 25 | $25/1120 \approx 0.0223$ |
| Wed | 1110 | 1120 | -10 | 100 | $100/1120 \approx 0.0893$ |
| Thu | 1120 | 1120 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Fri | 1126 | 1120 | 6 | 36 | $36/1120 \approx 0.0321$ |
| Sat | 1115 | 1120 | -5 | 25 | $25/1120 \approx 0.0223$ |
| **Total** | 6720 | 6720 | | | $\mathbf{\sum = 0.1803}$ |

$$\chi^2_{\text{Calculated}} = \mathbf{0.1803}$$

#### 3. Conclusion
* $\chi^2_{\text{Calculated}} = 0.1803$
* $\chi^2_{\text{Critical}} = 11.07$
* Since $0.1803 < 11.07$, the calculated $\chi^2$ falls within the acceptance region.

* **Result:** **Accept $H_0$** (Fail to Reject $H_0$).
* **Interpretation:** There is **no significant evidence** to suggest that the demand for the spare part varies significantly from day to day. The small differences observed are likely due to random chance.

### Example 2: Chi-Square Test for Independence (Ruler vs. Urban)

**Question:** Examine whether the **nature of area (Ruler/Urban) related to the voting preference** (A/B) in elections. (Assume $\alpha=0.05$).

| Area | Voter for A | Voter for B | Total |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ruler | $O=620$ | $O=380$ | 1000 |
| Urban | $O=550$ | $O=450$ | 1000 |
| Total | 1170 | 830 | 2000 |

#### 1. Hypotheses and Critical Value
* **$H_0$:** Area and voting preference are **independent** (not related).
* **$H_1$:** Area and voting preference are **dependent** (related).
* **Degrees of Freedom ($df$):** $df = (\text{Rows}-1) \times (\text{Columns}-1) = (2-1) \times (2-1) = 1$.
* **Critical Value ($\chi^2_{\text{Critical}}$):** For $df=1$ and $\alpha=0.05$, $\chi^2_{\text{Critical}} \approx \mathbf{3.84}$.

#### 2. Calculate Expected Frequencies ($E$)
$$E = \frac{(\text{Row Total}) \times (\text{Column Total})}{\text{Grand Total}}$$

| Cell (O) | Calculation (E) | Expected ($E$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ruler, Voter A (620) | $(1000 \times 1170) / 2000$ | 585 |
| Ruler, Voter B (380) | $(1000 \times 830) / 2000$ | 415 |
| Urban, Voter A (550) | $(1000 \times 1170) / 2000$ | 585 |
| Urban, Voter B (450) | $(1000 \times 830) / 2000$ | 415 |

#### 3. Calculate the $\chi^2$ Statistic
| Cell | $O$ | $E$ | $(O-E)$ | $(O-E)^2$ | $\frac{(O-E)^2}{E}$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| R, A | 620 | 585 | 35 | 1225 | $1225/585 \approx 2.0940$ |
| R, B | 380 | 415 | -35 | 1225 | $1225/415 \approx 2.9518$ |
| U, A | 550 | 585 | -35 | 1225 | $1225/585 \approx 2.0940$ |
| U, B | 450 | 415 | 35 | 1225 | $1225/415 \approx 2.9518$ |
| **Total** | 2000 | 2000 | | | $\mathbf{\sum = 10.0916}$ |

$$\chi^2_{\text{Calculated}} = \mathbf{10.0916}$$

#### 4. Conclusion
* $\chi^2_{\text{Calculated}} = 10.0916$
* $\chi^2_{\text{Critical}} = 3.84$
* Since $10.0916 > 3.84$, the calculated $\chi^2$ falls into the rejection region.

* **Result:** **Reject $H_0$**.
* **Interpretation:** There is a **significant relationship** between the nature of the area and voting preference. The variables are **dependent**.

### Example 3: Chi-Square Test for Independence (Inoculation Effectiveness)

**Question:** Use $\chi^{2}$ test to defend or refute the statement that the **inoculation prevents attack from cholera**. (Assume $\alpha=0.05$).

| | Attacked (A) | Not Attacked (NA) | Total |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Inoculated (I) | $O=30$ | $O=160$ | 190 |
| Not Inoculated (NI) | $O=140$ | $O=460$ | 600 |
| Total | 170 | 620 | 790 |

#### 1. Hypotheses and Critical Value
* **$H_0$:** Inoculation and being attacked are **independent** (Inoculation has no effect).
* **$H_1$:** Inoculation and being attacked are **dependent** (Inoculation prevents attack).
* **Degrees of Freedom ($df$):** $df = (2-1) \times (2-1) = 1$.
* **Critical Value ($\chi^2_{\text{Critical}}$):** For $df=1$ and $\alpha=0.05$, $\chi^2_{\text{Critical}} \approx \mathbf{3.84}$.

#### 2. Calculate Expected Frequencies ($E$)
| Cell | Calculation (E) | Expected ($E$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| I, A | $(190 \times 170) / 790$ | 40.9 |
| I, NA | $(190 \times 620) / 790$ | 149.1 |
| NI, A | $(600 \times 170) / 790$ | 129.1 |
| NI, NA | $(600 \times 620) / 790$ | 470.9 |

#### 3. Calculate the $\chi^2$ Statistic
| Cell | $O$ | $E$ | $(O-E)$ | $(O-E)^2$ | $\frac{(O-E)^2}{E}$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| I, A | 30 | 40.9 | -10.9 | 118.81 | $118.81/40.9 \approx 2.905$ |
| I, NA | 160 | 149.1 | 10.9 | 118.81 | $118.81/149.1 \approx 0.797$ |
| NI, A | 140 | 129.1 | 10.9 | 118.81 | $118.81/129.1 \approx 0.920$ |
| NI, NA | 460 | 470.9 | -10.9 | 118.81 | $118.81/470.9 \approx 0.252$ |
| **Total** | 790 | 790 | | | $\mathbf{\sum = 4.874}$ |

$$\chi^2_{\text{Calculated}} = \mathbf{4.874}$$

#### 4. Conclusion
* $\chi^2_{\text{Calculated}} = 4.874$
* $\chi^2_{\text{Critical}} = 3.84$
* Since $4.874 > 3.84$, the calculated $\chi^2$ falls into the rejection region.

* **Result:** **Reject $H_0$**.
* **Interpretation:** The statement is **defended**. There is a **significant relationship** between inoculation status and being attacked. Inoculation appears to be effective in preventing cholera attacks.

## Quiz and Assignment Questions Solutions

### Quiz 1: Chi-Square Goodness-of-Fit Test (Ratio)

**Question:** 500 students. Observed: 220 failed, 170 third, 90 second, rest first. Test if figures support the ratio **4:3:2:1**. ($\alpha=0.05$)

#### 1. Calculate Observed ($O$) and Expected ($E$) Frequencies
* **Total Sample:** $n = 500$.
* **Observed Frequencies ($O$):**
    * Failed: 220
    * Third: 170
    * Second: 90
    * First: $500 - (220+170+90) = 20$
* **Ratio Total:** $4+3+2+1 = 10$.
* **Expected Frequencies ($E$):**
    * Failed: $500 \times (4/10) = 200$
    * Third: $500 \times (3/10) = 150$
    * Second: $500 \times (2/10) = 100$
    * First: $500 \times (1/10) = 50$

#### 2. Hypotheses and Critical Value
* **$H_0$:** The categories are in the ratio $4:3:2:1$.
* **$H_1$:** The categories are not in the ratio $4:3:2:1$.
* **Degrees of Freedom ($df$):** $df = 4 - 1 = 3$.
* **Critical Value ($\chi^2_{\text{Critical}}$):** For $df=3$ and $\alpha=0.05$, $\chi^2_{\text{Critical}} \approx \mathbf{7.815}$.

#### 3. Calculate the $\chi^2$ Statistic
| Category | $O$ | $E$ | $(O-E)$ | $(O-E)^2$ | $\frac{(O-E)^2}{E}$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Failed | 220 | 200 | 20 | 400 | $400/200 = 2.00$ |
| Third | 170 | 150 | 20 | 400 | $400/150 \approx 2.67$ |
| Second | 90 | 100 | -10 | 100 | $100/100 = 1.00$ |
| First | 20 | 50 | -30 | 900 | $900/50 = 18.00$ |
| **Total** | 500 | 500 | | | $\mathbf{\sum = 23.67}$ |

$$\chi^2_{\text{Calculated}} = \mathbf{23.67}$$

#### 4. Conclusion
* $\chi^2_{\text{Calculated}} = 23.67$
* $\chi^2_{\text{Critical}} = 7.815$
* Since $23.67 > 7.815$, **Reject $H_0$**.
* **Answer:** The figures **do not support** the general belief that the categories are in the ratio $4:3:2:1$.

### Quiz 2: Chi-Square Test for Independence (Opinion vs. Class Level)

**Question:** 400 UG and 400 PG students. 290 UG favored, 310 PG favored. Test if **opinion is independent of class level**. ($\alpha=0.05$).

#### 1. Create Contingency Table and Hypotheses
| Class Level | Favored (F) | Not Favored (NF) | Total |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| UG | $O=290$ | $400-290=110$ | 400 |
| PG | $O=310$ | $400-310=90$ | 400 |
| Total | 600 | 200 | 800 |

* **$H_0$:** Opinion is **independent** of class level.
* **$H_1$:** Opinion is **dependent** on class level.
* **Degrees of Freedom ($df$):** $df = (2-1) \times (2-1) = 1$.
* **Critical Value ($\chi^2_{\text{Critical}}$):** For $df=1$ and $\alpha=0.05$, $\chi^2_{\text{Critical}} \approx \mathbf{3.84}$.

#### 2. Calculate Expected Frequencies ($E$)
Since row totals are equal (400) and column totals are 600 and 200:
* $E(\text{UG, F}) = E(\text{PG, F}) = (400 \times 600) / 800 = \mathbf{300}$
* $E(\text{UG, NF}) = E(\text{PG, NF}) = (400 \times 200) / 800 = \mathbf{100}$

#### 3. Calculate the $\chi^2$ Statistic
| Cell | $O$ | $E$ | $(O-E)$ | $(O-E)^2$ | $\frac{(O-E)^2}{E}$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| UG, F | 290 | 300 | -10 | 100 | $100/300 \approx 0.333$ |
| UG, NF | 110 | 100 | 10 | 100 | $100/100 = 1.000$ |
| PG, F | 310 | 300 | 10 | 100 | $100/300 \approx 0.333$ |
| PG, NF | 90 | 100 | -10 | 100 | $100/100 = 1.000$ |
| **Total** | 800 | 800 | | | $\mathbf{\sum = 2.666}$ |

$$\chi^2_{\text{Calculated}} = \mathbf{2.666}$$

#### 4. Conclusion
* $\chi^2_{\text{Calculated}} = 2.666$
* $\chi^2_{\text{Critical}} = 3.84$
* Since $2.666 < 3.84$, **Accept $H_0$**.
* **Answer:** Opinion regarding autonomous status of colleges is **independent** of the level of classes of students.
