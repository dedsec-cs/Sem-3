## Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)

**Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)** is a statistical test used to compare the means of **three or more** independent groups to determine if at least one group mean is significantly different from the others.

---

### When to Use ANOVA

ANOVA is essentially an extension of the **$t$-test** for situations involving more than two groups.

* **Goal:** To determine if the variation **between** the group means is larger than the variation **within** the groups.
* **Outcome Variable:** The variable being measured (e.g., test scores, crop yield) must be a **continuous** variable (interval or ratio data).
* **Factor/Grouping Variable:** The variable that defines the groups (e.g., treatment type, gender, location) must be **categorical**.
* **Alternative to Multiple $t$-tests:** Instead of running multiple pairwise $t$-tests (which increases the chance of making a Type I error—a false positive), ANOVA provides a single, comprehensive test.

---

### How to Use ANOVA (General Procedure)

ANOVA works by calculating the **F-statistic** ($\boldsymbol{F_{\text{Calculated}}}$), which is the ratio of two different variance estimates:

$$F = \frac{\text{Variance Between Samples (Mean Square Between)}}{\text{Variance Within Samples (Mean Square Within)}}$$

1.  **Mean Square Between ($\mathbf{MS_{B}}$):** Measures the variability *between* the different group means. It represents the effect of the treatment or factor.
2.  **Mean Square Within ($\mathbf{MS_{W}}$):** Measures the variability *within* each individual group. It represents random error or chance variation.

If the calculated $F$-value is **significantly large** (meaning $MS_B$ is much larger than $MS_W$), we **reject the null hypothesis**, concluding that the groups are not all the same.

---

## Hypothesis Testing Procedure and Interpretation

### Hypotheses

ANOVA always tests the means of the groups being compared:

* **Null Hypothesis ($\mathbf{H_0}$):** All population means are equal.
    $$H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \mu_3 = \dots = \mu_k$$
* **Alternative Hypothesis ($\mathbf{H_1}$):** At least one population mean is different from the others.
    $$H_1: \text{Not all means are equal.}$$

### Interpreting ANOVA Results

The final decision is made by comparing $F_{\text{Calculated}}$ to $F_{\text{Critical}}$ (or by using the P-value).

| Comparison | Result | Interpretation | Next Step |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$F_{\text{Calculated}} \le F_{\text{Critical}}$** (P-value $> \alpha$) | **Accept $H_0$** | There is **no significant evidence** that the group means are different. | Stop: Conclude the factor has no effect. |
| **$F_{\text{Calculated}} > F_{\text{Critical}}$** (P-value $\le \alpha$) | **Reject $H_0$** | **At least one group mean is significantly different.** | Proceed to **Post-Hoc Tests** to find out *which* specific pairs are different (e.g., Tukey's HSD). |

---

## One-Factor vs. Two-Factor ANOVA

The classification of ANOVA depends on the number of **categorical factors** (independent variables) being tested in the model.

### 1. One-Factor ANOVA (One-Way ANOVA)

* **Definition:** Used when comparing the means of groups created by **one single factor**.
* **Example:** A researcher tests three different teaching methods (Method A, Method B, Method C) on student test scores.
    * *Outcome Variable:* Test Score (Continuous)
    * *Factor:* Teaching Method (Categorical, 3 levels)

### 2. Two-Factor ANOVA (Two-Way ANOVA)

* **Definition:** Used when comparing means based on **two independent factors** simultaneously.
* **Advantage:** It allows you to test for the separate effect of each factor **and** the **interaction effect** between the two factors.
* **Example:** A researcher tests three different teaching methods (Factor A) and two different class sizes (Factor B: Large, Small) on student test scores.
    * *Factor A:* Teaching Method
    * *Factor B:* Class Size
    * *Interaction:* Does the best teaching method depend on the class size?

---

## Identifying the Appropriate Hypothesis Testing Procedure

The choice of the correct test depends on the nature of the data and the number of samples being compared.

| Scenario                                    | Outcome Variable Type | Number of Samples (Groups)    | Appropriate Hypothesis Test                                                                 |
| :------------------------------------------ | :-------------------- | :---------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Comparing a Sample to a Claim**           | Continuous            | 1                             | **$Z$-test** ($n \ge 30$ or $\sigma$ known) or **$t$-test** ($n < 30$ and $\sigma$ unknown) |
| **Comparing Two Groups**                    | Continuous            | 2                             | **$Z$-test** (if large samples) or **$t$-test** (if small samples)                          |
| **Comparing Multiple Groups (3 or more)**   | Continuous            | $k \ge 3$                     | **One-Factor ANOVA** (or Two-Factor ANOVA, etc.)                                            |
| **Testing Relationship between Categories** | Categorical           | 1 (single contingency table)  | **Chi-Square Test for Independence**                                                        |
| **Testing Frequency Distribution**          | Categorical           | 1 (single set of frequencies) | **Chi-Square Goodness-of-Fit Test**                                                         |

## Example 1: One-Way ANOVA (Hospital Deaths)

**Question:** Compare **three Hospitals** (A, B, C) with regards to **number of deaths per month**. Suggest a difference in the no. of the deaths per month among three hospitals. (Assume $\alpha=0.05$)

| Hospital (Group) | Deaths ($x$) | $n_j$ | $\bar{x}_j$ | $\sum x_j$ | $\sum x_j^2$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A | 3, 4, 3, 5, 0 | 5 | 3.0 | 15 | 59 |
| B | 6, 3, 3, 4, 4 | 5 | 4.0 | 20 | 74 |
| C | 7, 3, 4, 6, 5 | 5 | 5.0 | 25 | 135 |
| **Totals** | | $N=15$ | $\bar{x}_{\text{Grand}} = 4.0$ | $\sum x = 60$ | $\sum x^2 = 268$ |

#### 1. Hypotheses
* $H_0: \mu_A = \mu_B = \mu_C$ (No difference in mean deaths per month.)
* $H_1:$ At least one mean is different.

#### 2. Calculate Sums of Squares

**A. Total Sum of Squares ($\mathbf{SS_T}$):** Measures total variability.
$$SS_T = \sum x^2 - \frac{(\sum x)^2}{N} = 268 - \frac{(60)^2}{15} = 268 - 240 = \mathbf{28}$$

**B. Between-Groups Sum of Squares ($\mathbf{SS_B}$):** Measures variability due to the hospitals.
$$SS_B = \sum \frac{(\sum x_j)^2}{n_j} - \frac{(\sum x)^2}{N} = \left( \frac{15^2}{5} + \frac{20^2}{5} + \frac{25^2}{5} \right) - 240$$
$$SS_B = (45 + 80 + 125) - 240 = 250 - 240 = \mathbf{10}$$

**C. Within-Groups Sum of Squares ($\mathbf{SS_W}$):** Measures variability due to error/chance.
$$SS_W = SS_T - SS_B = 28 - 10 = \mathbf{18}$$

#### 3. Calculate Mean Squares and F-Ratio
* $k = 3$ groups. $N = 15$ total observations.
* **$df_{\text{Between}}$** ($k-1$) $= 3 - 1 = 2$
* **$df_{\text{Within}}$** ($N-k$) $= 15 - 3 = 12$
* $\mathbf{MS_{B}} = \frac{SS_B}{df_B} = \frac{10}{2} = \mathbf{5.0}$
* $\mathbf{MS_{W}} = \frac{SS_W}{df_W} = \frac{18}{12} = \mathbf{1.5}$

$$\mathbf{F_{\text{Calculated}}} = \frac{MS_B}{MS_W} = \frac{5.0}{1.5} \approx \mathbf{3.33}$$

#### 4. Conclusion
* **Critical Value ($F_{\text{Critical}}$):** For $df=(2, 12)$ and $\alpha=0.05$, $F_{\text{Critical}} \approx \mathbf{3.89}$ (using common tables).
* $F_{\text{Calculated}} = 3.33$
* Since $3.33 < 3.89$, the calculated $F$ falls within the acceptance region.

* **Result:** **Accept $H_0$** (Fail to Reject $H_0$).
* **Interpretation:** There is **no significant difference** in the mean number of deaths per month among the three hospitals at the 5% level of significance.

## Example 2: One-Way ANOVA (Machine Speed)

**Question:** Determine whether the **three machines (A1, A2, A3)** are **significantly different in their mean speed**. (Given $F_{2,12}=3.89$)

| Machine (Group) | $n_j$ | $\bar{x}_j$ | $\sum x_j$ | $\sum x_j^2$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A1 | 5 | 32.0 | 160 | 5174 |
| A2 | 5 | 37.0 | 185 | 6843 |
| A3 | 5 | 29.0 | 145 | 4257 |
| **Totals** | $N=15$ | $\bar{x}_{\text{Grand}} = 32.67$ | $\sum x = 490$ | $\sum x^2 = 16274$ |

#### 1. Hypotheses
* $H_0: \mu_{A1} = \mu_{A2} = \mu_{A3}$ (No difference in mean speed.)
* $H_1:$ At least one mean is different.

#### 2. Calculate Sums of Squares

**A. Total Sum of Squares ($\mathbf{SS_T}$):**
$$SS_T = 16274 - \frac{(490)^2}{15} = 16274 - 16006.67 = \mathbf{267.33}$$

**B. Between-Groups Sum of Squares ($\mathbf{SS_B}$):**
$$SS_B = \left( \frac{160^2}{5} + \frac{185^2}{5} + \frac{145^2}{5} \right) - 16006.67$$
$$SS_B = (5120 + 6845 + 4205) - 16006.67 = 16170 - 16006.67 = \mathbf{163.33}$$

**C. Within-Groups Sum of Squares ($\mathbf{SS_W}$):**
$$SS_W = SS_T - SS_B = 267.33 - 163.33 = \mathbf{104.00}$$

#### 3. Calculate Mean Squares and F-Ratio
* $k=3$ groups. $N=15$ total observations.
* $df_{\text{Between}} = 2$
* $df_{\text{Within}} = 12$
* $\mathbf{MS_{B}} = \frac{163.33}{2} \approx \mathbf{81.67}$
* $\mathbf{MS_{W}} = \frac{104.00}{12} \approx \mathbf{8.67}$

$$\mathbf{F_{\text{Calculated}}} = \frac{MS_B}{MS_W} = \frac{81.67}{8.67} \approx \mathbf{9.42}$$

#### 4. Conclusion
* **Critical Value ($F_{\text{Critical}}$):** Given as $F_{2,12}= \mathbf{3.89}$.
* $F_{\text{Calculated}} = 9.42$
* Since $9.42 > 3.89$, the calculated $F$ falls into the rejection region.

* **Result:** **Reject $H_0$**.
* **Interpretation:** There is a **highly significant difference** in the mean speed among the three machines. We conclude that at least one machine is significantly faster or slower than the others.

### Assignment 1: One-Way ANOVA (Wheat Varieties)

**Question:** Test for **significant difference in the production of four varieties of wheat** (A, B, C, D). (The table layout suggests a Two-Way ANOVA, but since the question only asks about the varieties, we proceed with a **One-Way ANOVA** treating Plots as replications).

| Variety | $n_j$ | $\sum x_j$ | $\bar{x}_j$ | $\sum x_j^2$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A | 3 | 630 | 210 | 132400 |
| B | 3 | 650 | 216.67 | 148900 |
| C | 3 | 695 | 231.67 | 196825 |
| D | 3 | 750 | 250 | 229500 |
| **Totals** | $N=12$ | $\sum x = 2725$ | $\bar{x}_{\text{Grand}} = 227.08$ | $\sum x^2 = 707625$ |

* $F_{\text{Critical}}$ for varieties: $F_{3,8} = 4.07$ (since $k-1=3$ and $N-k=8$).

#### 1. Calculate Sums of Squares

* **Correction Factor (CF):** $\frac{(\sum x)^2}{N} = \frac{2725^2}{12} \approx 618,802.08$
* $\mathbf{SS_T} = 707625 - 618802.08 = \mathbf{88,822.92}$
* $\mathbf{SS_B} = \left( \frac{630^2}{3} + \frac{650^2}{3} + \frac{695^2}{3} + \frac{750^2}{3} \right) - 618802.08$
    $$SS_B = (132300 + 140833.33 + 160958.33 + 187500) - 618802.08 = 621591.66 - 618802.08 = \mathbf{2789.58}$$
* $\mathbf{SS_W} = SS_T - SS_B = 88822.92 - 2789.58 = \mathbf{86,033.34}$

#### 2. Calculate Mean Squares and F-Ratio
* $\mathbf{MS_{B}} = \frac{SS_B}{k-1} = \frac{2789.58}{3} \approx \mathbf{929.86}$
* $\mathbf{MS_{W}} = \frac{SS_W}{N-k} = \frac{86033.34}{8} \approx \mathbf{10,754.17}$

$$\mathbf{F_{\text{Calculated}}} = \frac{MS_B}{MS_W} = \frac{929.86}{10754.17} \approx \mathbf{0.0865}$$

#### 3. Conclusion
* $F_{\text{Calculated}} = 0.0865$
* $F_{\text{Critical}}$ for $(3, 8)$ d.f. $= \mathbf{4.07}$.
* Since $0.0865 < 4.07$, **Accept $H_0$**.
* **Answer:** There is **no significant difference** in the production of these four varieties of wheat.

### Assignment 2: One-Way ANOVA (Wheat Varieties A, B, C)

**Question:** Test for **significant difference in the production of three varieties A, B and C**. (Given $F_{2,9}=4.261$)

| Variety | Data ($x$) | $n_j$ | $\sum x_j$ | $\sum x_j^2$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A | 14, 16, 18 | 3 | 48 | 776 |
| B | 14, 13, 15, 22 | 4 | 64 | 1086 |
| C | 18, 16, 19, 19, 20 | 5 | 92 | 1702 |
| **Totals** | | $N=12$ | $\sum x = 204$ | $\sum x^2 = 3564$ |

* $F_{\text{Critical}}$ for varieties: $F_{2,9} = 4.261$ (since $k-1=2$ and $N-k=9$).

#### 1. Calculate Sums of Squares

* **Correction Factor (CF):** $\frac{(204)^2}{12} = 3468$
* $\mathbf{SS_T} = 3564 - 3468 = \mathbf{96}$
* $\mathbf{SS_B} = \left( \frac{48^2}{3} + \frac{64^2}{4} + \frac{92^2}{5} \right) - 3468$
    $$SS_B = (768 + 1024 + 1692.8) - 3468 = 3484.8 - 3468 = \mathbf{16.8}$$
* $\mathbf{SS_W} = 96 - 16.8 = \mathbf{79.2}$

#### 2. Calculate Mean Squares and F-Ratio
* $df_{\text{Between}} = 2$
* $df_{\text{Within}} = 9$
* $\mathbf{MS_{B}} = \frac{16.8}{2} = \mathbf{8.4}$
* $\mathbf{MS_{W}} = \frac{79.2}{9} = \mathbf{8.8}$

$$\mathbf{F_{\text{Calculated}}} = \frac{MS_B}{MS_W} = \frac{8.4}{8.8} \approx \mathbf{0.9545}$$

#### 3. Conclusion
* $F_{\text{Calculated}} = 0.9545$
* $F_{\text{Critical}}$ for $(2, 9)$ d.f. $= \mathbf{4.261}$.
* Since $0.9545 < 4.261$, **Accept $H_0$**.
* **Answer:** There is **no significant difference** in the production of the three varieties of wheat (A, B, and C).
